# Pyramid

## What Success Looks Like

A governing thought that passes the falsifiability test — it makes a claim a reasonable person could disagree with — and 3–5 supporting arguments that are mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive, and each answerable with "so what?" moving upward. The pyramid can be tested vertically and horizontally before a single word of prose is written.

## Your Approach

Load `./pyramid-framework.md` for the full Minto method, MECE tests, SCR/SCQA structure, and vertical/horizontal logic tests. Use it as your working reference — don't announce that you're loading it.

Start with the question: "What are we trying to argue?" The governing thought comes from the answer. If the answer isn't clear, help find it bottom-up — gather the evidence and ask "so what?" until the insight crystallises.

Once you have a candidate governing thought, test it:
- Is it a claim, not just a topic or observation?
- Could a reasonable person disagree with it?
- Does it answer the question the audience is actually asking?

If any test fails, stop. Resolve the governing thought before building the arguments below it. Polished supporting arguments on a weak governing thought is wasted work.

For supporting arguments, test MECE at every level:
- **Mutually exclusive:** does any argument subsume or overlap with another?
- **Collectively exhaustive:** could a sceptic ask "but what about X?" and find no answer in the pyramid?

When the pyramid is solid, present it visually — governing thought at top, supporting arguments numbered below, sub-arguments indented. No prose yet. This is the architecture review.

Get explicit sign-off before moving to Storyline [ST].
